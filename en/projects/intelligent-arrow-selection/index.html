Research and Development of Third-Generation Intelligent Arrow Selection Equipment and Systems

A multimodal research workflow connecting arrow measurements, high-speed video and target images to assess shot dispersion and stability across rounds.

Role

May 2025 – May 2026

The research question

How can physical measurements and visual evidence help identify arrows that perform consistently, even when their nominal specifications are the same?

My contribution

  1. Curated and manually validated a dataset linking arrow measurements, shooting trials, target images, impact coordinates and ring scores.
  2. Extracted launch velocity and angle from high-speed video and analysed their relationship with impact patterns.
  3. Led the fine-tuning and integration of SAM 3.1 and YOLO11s for archery target segmentation and arrow-impact localisation.

How it works

  1. 01Structured experiment records connect arrow identifiers, trial rounds and manually checked impact observations.
  2. 02Target segmentation and impact localisation translate images into measurements for dispersion and stability analysis.
  3. 03Offline temporal fusion combines observations across a sequence to improve detection of arrow impacts.
  4. 04Manual review keeps the resulting measurements traceable to the original target images.

Evaluation & findings

92.58%Precision · offline fusion
88.51%Recall · offline fusion
90.50%F1 · offline fusion

Offline temporal fusion achieved 92.58% precision, 88.51% recall and 90.50% F1 for impact localisation in a development evaluation of 99 images, 20 sequences and 296 impact observations. The observations include arrows appearing in multiple frames.

Reliable arrow selection starts with consistent experiment records and well-defined measurements. Impact localisation, ring scoring and physical shot dispersion each describe a different part of the system.

Research output

Co-author of “Arrow Selection for Elite Series Arrows: Data Driven Archery Arrow Selection”, under peer review at Sports Engineering.
