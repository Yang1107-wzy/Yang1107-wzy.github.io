Research and Development of Third-Generation Intelligent Arrow Selection Equipment and Systems

A multimodal research workflow connecting arrow measurements, high-speed video and target images to assess shot dispersion and stability across rounds.

Research Background

Competition arrows with the same nominal specifications can differ in their flight and impact patterns. This research investigates how physical measurements, controlled shooting trials and visual observations can be combined to assess consistency within an arrow group and stability across rounds.

The work connects experimental data management with computer vision and statistical analysis. Arrow-impact localisation supplies measurements for the wider selection study, while high-speed video provides shot-level kinematic information that can be compared with the observed target patterns.

Research Question

How can physical measurements and visual evidence help identify arrows that perform consistently, even when their nominal specifications are the same?

Individual Contributions

  • Curated and manually validated a dataset linking arrow measurements, shooting trials, target images, impact coordinates and ring scores.
  • Extracted launch velocity and angle from high-speed video and analysed their relationship with impact patterns.
  • Led the fine-tuning and integration of SAM 3.1 and YOLO11s for archery target segmentation and arrow-impact localisation.

Data & Study Design

Experimental records link arrow identifiers and physical measurements with trial rounds, target images, impact coordinates and ring scores. Manual validation maintains these correspondences so that comparisons of dispersion and cross-round stability can be traced back to the original observations.

The offline localisation evaluation contains 99 target images from 20 sequences and 296 impact observations. A physical arrow may appear in several frames. The reported precision, recall and F1 describe impact localisation under offline temporal fusion, which can use later frames in a sequence; they are distinct from ring-scoring accuracy.

Research Methods

  1. 01Associate arrow measurements, shooting rounds, target images and manually verified impact coordinates in structured experimental records.
  2. 02Extract launch velocity and angle from high-speed video and compare these kinematic measurements with impact dispersion and stability.
  3. 03Integrate SAM 3.1 and YOLO11s for target segmentation and impact localisation, converting image observations into measurable coordinates.
  4. 04Combine detections across a sequence using offline temporal fusion, then retain manually reviewed links between measurements and source images.

Evaluation & Findings

92.58%Precision · offline fusion
88.51%Recall · offline fusion
90.50%F1 · offline fusion

Offline temporal fusion achieved 92.58% precision, 88.51% recall and 90.50% F1 for impact localisation in a development evaluation of 99 images, 20 sequences and 296 impact observations. The observations include arrows appearing in multiple frames.

Discussion

The study distinguishes measurement quality from arrow-selection outcomes. Detection precision and recall evaluate the visual component; dispersion, ring scores and stability across rounds describe related but separate aspects of shooting performance.
