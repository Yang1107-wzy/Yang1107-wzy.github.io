Hydrological Constraints on Post-Fire Vegetation Recovery

An analysis of modelled soil water and MODIS NDVI using the 2019–2020 Gospers Mountain megafire as a natural experiment.

Role
Hydrological and vegetation data analysis · Second author
Period
Jun 2026 – Sep 2026
Focus

The research question

How do long-term soil-water conditions align with vegetation recovery after major disturbance, and does that relationship also appear in year-to-year changes?

My contribution

  1. Analysed modelled soil-water saturation and MODIS NDVI across equal six-year periods before and after the fire.
  2. Compared positive soil-water–NDVI trend concordance before disturbance and during the 2020–2025 recovery period.
  3. Examined year-to-year changes separately from long-term trends.

How it works

  1. 01Use the Gospers Mountain megafire as the disturbance event for a pre/post comparison.
  2. 02Pair modelled hydrological conditions with a satellite-derived vegetation index over matched time spans.
  3. 03Compare the direction of long-term soil-water and vegetation trends.
  4. 04Analyse annual changes separately to distinguish persistent trends from short-term coupling.

Evaluation & findings

6 + 6Years · equal pre/post-fire windows
37.8%Positive trend concordance · before
95.3%Positive trend concordance · recovery

Positive soil-water–NDVI trend concordance increased from 37.8% before the fire to 95.3% during 2020–2025 recovery. Year-to-year changes did not show positive coupling.

The timescale changes the interpretation of the relationship. Strong agreement between long-term trends can coexist with an absence of positive coupling in annual changes.

Research output

Second author of “Long-term hydrological support constrains vegetation recovery after major disturbance”; abstract accepted for in-person poster presentation at BES Annual Meeting 2026.
