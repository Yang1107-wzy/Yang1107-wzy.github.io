MapAgent: Indoor Localisation and Spatial Reasoning

A Qwen3-based localisation agent that selects candidate positions using BLE, motion and map evidence, with independent verification and deterministic fallback.

Research Background

Indoor localisation must resolve ambiguous wireless observations within the constraints of a known environment. MapAgent investigates how an LLM-based agent can use BLE, motion and map evidence to select a candidate position, while an independent verification stage checks the proposed decision.

The project is my final year project at BNBU under the supervision of Xiubin Fan. Its core implementation separates candidate generation, evidence-based selection and deterministic verification so that the accepted position and the fallback decision follow explicit constraints.

Research Question

How can an indoor-localisation agent reason over ambiguous wireless observations while keeping its decisions within the constraints of a known map?

Individual Contributions

  • Developed the core localisation pipeline and its evaluation workflow as a final year project.
  • Connected BLE observations, motion evidence and supplied map geometry with Qwen3-based candidate selection.
  • Built a decision path with independent verification and deterministic fallback.
  • Evaluated the system across six sites and 41 trajectories.

Data & Study Design

The reported evaluation covers six sites and 41 trajectories, using supplied map geometry and anchor layouts. The map and anchor information are part of the input setting; the result does not assume that the same evaluation also reconstructs a map automatically from an arbitrary floor-plan image.

The verified agent’s 3.317 m error is the average of the trajectory-level mean localisation errors, giving each trajectory equal weight. Candidate-selection and deterministic methods are assessed on the same trajectory group; geometric validity and localisation accuracy are separate evaluation criteria.

Research Methods

  1. 01Construct a bounded set of candidate positions from BLE observations, motion context and supplied map constraints.
  2. 02Provide Qwen3 with the registered candidates and structured evidence for the location-selection decision.
  3. 03Check the proposed choice independently against geometric, reachability and evidence constraints.
  4. 04Use a deterministic fallback when a proposal fails verification, and evaluate the resulting positions against trajectory ground truth.

Evaluation & Findings

Across six sites and 41 trajectories, the verified agent obtained a trajectory-averaged localisation error of 3.317 m. The evaluation used supplied map geometry and anchor layouts.

Discussion

Verification provides an explicit decision contract, but a geometrically valid and well-supported choice can still have a larger position error. The study therefore evaluates constraint compliance and location accuracy separately, using deterministic choices as a reference.
