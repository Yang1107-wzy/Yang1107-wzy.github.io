Cardiac Segmentation and Spatiotemporal Modelling

A study of temporal recalibration and cyclic trajectory regularisation for 2D+t cine MRI segmentation under ED/ES-only supervision.

Research Background

Cardiac cine MRI records anatomy through a repeating cardiac cycle, while expert segmentation labels may be available only at end diastole and end systole. The research examines how temporal feature recalibration and trajectory-based regularisation affect segmentation under this sparse-supervision setting.

ST-Net uses a shared two-dimensional segmentation backbone with a bottleneck Temporal Attention Gate. A second-order cyclic regulariser is computed from predicted area and centroid trajectories, allowing the study to examine spatial segmentation and temporal behaviour within the same framework.

Research Question

When expert labels cover only end diastole and end systole, how do temporal feature modules and trajectory constraints affect cardiac segmentation?

Individual Contributions

  • Co-developed ST-Net for 2D+t cine MRI segmentation with a bottleneck Temporal Attention Gate.
  • Implemented second-order cyclic regularisation of predicted area and centroid trajectories.
  • Benchmarked seven model and loss configurations on 50 ACDC cine MRI cases using five-fold patient-level cross-validation.

Data & Study Design

The study compares seven model and loss configurations on 50 ACDC cine MRI cases using patient-level five-fold cross-validation. Each fold contains 35 training, five validation and ten test cases; expert ED/ES labels provide the segmentation supervision.

Each slice is represented as a 2D+t sequence with 16 resampled cardiac phases at 128 × 128 pixels. Comparisons consider spatial segmentation, area and centroid trajectories, and functional measurements. Patient-level splits keep frames from the same patient within the same partition.

Research Methods

  1. 01Apply a shared two-dimensional U-Net encoder and decoder to the cardiac phases in each sequence.
  2. 02Recalibrate bottleneck features across time using a Temporal Attention Gate with residual feature adjustment.
  3. 03Compute area and centroid trajectories from predicted segmentation probabilities and regularise their second-order differences with cyclic boundary conditions.
  4. 04Compare seven combinations of model components and loss functions under a common patient-level protocol and ED/ES-only segmentation supervision.

Seven configurations were compared on 50 ACDC cases using patient-level five-fold cross-validation and ED/ES-only labels. The study examines the effects of temporal modules and loss choices under the same supervision and split design.

Discussion

Spatial boundary quality, temporal variation and functional measurements describe different aspects of a cine segmentation model. Smoother trajectories or larger contraction amplitudes do not by themselves establish anatomically correct intermediate phases, for which this supervision setting has no expert labels.
