Spatiotemporal Flood Forecasting and Hydrological Modelling

A hydrological modelling project combining river-network structure, gauge observations and reanalysis data to study river-discharge forecasting.

Research Background

River discharge varies over time and is influenced by conditions across connected upstream and downstream locations. This research combines hydrological time series with spatial river-network information to represent these relationships in regional flood forecasting.

The work forms part of the Guangdong Major Project of Basic and Applied Basic Research, “High-Resolution Simulation and Prediction of the Land Surface Environment” (Project No. 2021B0301030007). The modelling study addresses both the integration of heterogeneous environmental observations and the evaluation of high-flow behaviour.

Research Question

How can temporal observations and river-network structure be combined to represent downstream propagation and improve the treatment of high-flow events?

Individual Contributions

  • Developed a flow-direction-aware river-discharge forecasting method combining hydrological time series and spatial river-network graphs.
  • Integrated ERA5-Land, 75 gauges across 15 rivers and static geospatial rasters into river-specific 29-channel inputs.
  • Implemented event-aware raster-Transformer training with river-balanced regression and penalties for high-flow underprediction.
  • Assessed three-seed ensembles with chronological data splits and validation-based checkpoint selection.

Data & Study Design

Inputs combine ERA5-Land reanalysis, hydrometric observations from 75 gauges across 15 rivers, and static geospatial rasters. Each river is represented by a 29-channel grid containing meteorological, geographical and hydrological context.

Water-level observations are forward-filled for at most seven days, with observation age retained; target discharge is not interpolated by this procedure. Evaluation uses chronological splits, checkpoint selection on validation data and ensembles of three neural training seeds. Experiments with future reanalysis inputs are distinct from a forecast restricted to information available at issue time.

Research Methods

  1. 01Organise environmental and hydrometric information into separate spatial grids for each river, retaining flow direction and river-network context.
  2. 02Apply forward-only filling to water levels with a seven-day age limit, while keeping the discharge target and the age of observations explicit.
  3. 03Train a raster Transformer with river-balanced regression, event-aware weighting and an additional penalty for underestimating high flows.
  4. 04Select checkpoints on validation performance and evaluate ensembles of three training seeds under the stated chronological and input-information conditions.

The research integrates 15 rivers, 75 gauges and 29-channel inputs, with evaluation of three-seed ensembles. The related abstract was accepted for poster presentation at WRE 2026 in Seoul in December 2026.

Discussion

Flood-forecasting evaluation depends on which information is available at prediction time and how performance is distributed across rivers and high-flow events. River-balanced objectives and underprediction penalties provide complementary views of model behaviour beyond an aggregate regression score.
